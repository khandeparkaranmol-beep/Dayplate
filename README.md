# Dayplate – Personal Health Tracker

[Open Dayplate on GitHub Pages](https://khandeparkaranmol-beep.github.io/Dayplate/)

A mobile-friendly food, activity and weight journal. Choose Sign in with Google to sync across devices or Use offline to keep a diary in this browser. Both experiences open on GitHub Pages. Each account has its own diary. A username lets you invite friends and compete without publishing your private health records.

## Use Dayplate

- **Journal** — log Maharashtrian, Goan and Continental dishes, Indian packaged foods and protein shakes. Pick a portion, see the nutrient totals and repeat a past meal. Each logged meal shows protein and total sugar by default. Choose **Meal nutrients** under **Your meals** to select nutrients such as carbs, fat, fiber and sodium. Set the columns per row or let them fit your screen. Tap any meal for its per-food breakdown. Open a row under **Macronutrient breakdown** to see the meals that contributed to the daily total. Partial totals show how many foods supplied a value.
- **Indian biscuits** — filter Foods or Log food by Indian biscuits to browse 58 named products. Piece weights are specific to the biscuit and pack. Where a count is unavailable, use the labelled serving or enter grams. “Dark Magic” shows named chocolate biscuit options and a way to enter your own label.
- **Foods** — browse 21,182 built-in items, including 119 packaged products and 20 Mumbai and Goan café dishes with recipe estimates for total and added sugars. Six of these dishes also have a Qmin Panaji menu entry for published calories. The Indian breakfast, lunch and snack filters include 120 additional regional recipes. Search by common names such as tomato omelette, besan chilla, rajma chawal or ragda pattice. Inspect each source, portion basis and missing nutrients in the food data audit. Home-cooked recipes use explicit estimates.
- **Indian fast food** — choose a restaurant or search for an order. The 958 menu entries cover McDonald’s (207 West & South, 89 North & East), KFC (196), Burger King (99), Pizza Hut (297), Domino’s (53) and Wow! Momo (17). Entries distinguish sizes, crusts and the portion you can log. Restaurant availability varies. Each record links to its Indian menu source. Undeclared nutrients stay unknown, and conflicting menu rows are held out.
- **Soups** — find 44 soup recipes in Foods or Log food. Choose cream of chicken, cream of mutton (goat), cream of lamb, Goan caldo verde, Maharashtrian tomato saar, shorba and more. Log a bowl, a fraction or grams. Each new recipe lists the ingredients and cooked serving weight behind its estimates.
- **Indian kebabs** — browse 60 new recipes alongside the existing paneer and tofu tikkas. Log a piece, skewer, patty or weighed portion. Chicken, goat (mutton) and lamb have separate tikka, seekh and galouti entries. The nutrition panel lists ingredient weights per 100 g of cooked edible food, source references and the assumptions behind each estimate. Bones and sides are excluded. The soya chaap base uses the Desi Treat label, which omits potassium.
- **US restaurants and drinks** — choose a chain and a menu source. The US catalog contains 19,578 portions from 76 chains, including 7,484 newer menu records and 12,094 records from the 2018 MenuStat archive. Sizes, sweetness levels, toppings and meal components can have separate entries. The year beside each serving identifies its published guide or snapshot. Undated live menus use the year checked. Local availability varies.
- **Dallas / DFW** — browse 22 regional dishes alongside selected chains serving the area. Look for brisket tacos with gravy, chile con queso, migas breakfast tacos and Texas State Fair corn dogs. Regional dishes use documented ingredient weights and include the oil, sides or toppings named in the portion.
- **Indo-Chinese, Tofu, Ghanaian and American** — 122 additional dishes include Hakka noodles, chilli tofu, waakye and buttermilk pancakes. The audit records ingredients, serving weights and preparation assumptions. US recipes use USDA SR28. Paneer uses IFCT and cooked whole-maize kenkey uses the FAO/INFOODS Western Africa table.
- **Menu sources and audit** — individual records link to the applicable restaurant guide or research dataset. Missing nutrients stay unknown. The separate review checked serving units, source identity and nutrient values, and held out unresolved conflicts. The [2018 MenuStat dataset](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/K4NYTR) supplies the archive. [PrintSheetLab’s October 2026 Starbucks compilation](https://printsheetlab.com/guides/starbucks-nutrition-data/) supplies the newer Starbucks drinks.

- **Individual items** — search for a whole avocado, tomato, pav, biscuit or Goan sungtache dangar. Choose a count, a fractional piece or grams. The tomato and pav also offer a slice unit. Each portion lists its reference weight and source under Nutrition facts.

- **Movement** — choose a catalog activity and duration from pickers to see active calories above rest. Estimates use your workout duration, a matching activity from the 2024 Adult Compendium and body weight. Apple Health workout energy takes priority when available. Tap a session to review its estimate or supply missing details. You can also import Apple's Health export. On iPhone, go to Health → profile → Export All Health Data and save the file. In Dayplate choose Movement → Import Apple Health. Pick dates and sources, preview the result, and save.
- **Progress** — review calorie targets, estimated resting energy needs, weight, measurements and activity over time.
- **Share my day** — open the selected Journal date, preview logged meals, food totals, workouts and available activity, then create a read-only link for WhatsApp or another messenger. Choose **Live day link** to show the latest saved entries for that diary date whenever the link opens, or **Fixed snapshot** to keep the preview as shared. Both work for seven days for anyone who receives the link. Live views also refresh each minute while visible. Existing snapshot links keep their original content. Turn it off under **Active links for this day**. Offline diaries can send a text recap. Public links open the lightweight `share.html` page.
- **Strength and rowing** — select dumbbell and kettlebell movements or rowing pace in Movement. Record optional sets, reps and kilograms per weight. Calorie estimates use duration and body weight. Isolation lifts use a general resistance-training estimate.
- **Friends** — claim a username, send a request, then invite an accepted friend to a 3-, 7- or 14-day challenge for steps, workout days, workout minutes or completed food-journal days. Both people see the metric and duration before accepting. The scoreboard shows only totals. Use **Share from diary** to choose a logged date, preview a meal, individual food, workout or step count, and share it with one friend or all current friends.

### Plan, log and review

1. Open **Foods → My kitchen → New recipe**. Enter ingredients for the full batch, then set the yield, such as four katoris. Save a single ingredient with a yield of one to define a personal ladle, cup or dabba. A portion carries a nutrient estimate only when all ingredients supply that nutrient.
2. In the Journal, choose **Plan my next meal**. Adjust quantities to preview the plate and its effect on your logged day. Choose **Compare another plate** to hold one option beside the current edit. Switch between the plates before saving or logging the one you want. **Save for later** keeps a private draft. **Ate this** logs the portions together. Editing a recipe later preserves the recipe captured in previous meals.
3. Open **Progress → Your weekly review**. Inspect completed days, compare logged breakfasts, review weight and waist measurements, and see your chosen nutrients. Use the date selector to revisit a week. **Food variety this week** lets you record the groups present in mixed meals.
4. In **Progress → Breakfast experiment**, choose two usual breakfasts. After having one, rate afternoon hunger from 1 to 5. Both breakfasts show their rating counts and averages. You can edit a rating on its diary date.
5. In **Movement → Log workout**, select boxing and enable **Record boxing rounds** to save rounds, length, rest and perceived effort alongside the workout's active calories.

### Apple Health and Fitness

The browser processes export.zip or export.xml on-device. It saves only the selected daily activity summaries and workouts after confirmation. Reimport to refresh. Use one source per metric to avoid summing overlapping phone and watch records. For active energy, the exported Apple Fitness Move summary takes precedence. When both active and resting calories are present the site also shows their sum. Missing data is displayed as a dash.

A web browser cannot request native HealthKit access or read the Apple Watch in the background. Imported steps can differ from Apple's merged data. Source-level totals use the start date written into each sample. Imported workouts keep stable identifiers across repeat imports. A later export does not remove diary records omitted from it.

### Account privacy

Private journal records use the signed-in account identifier on every read and write. New accounts have no public username until they choose one. Friend requests and challenges require acceptance. Only diary entries you explicitly share appear in the feed. Body weight and calorie targets stay private. Your list of friends and requests is visible only to you. Each shared entry is a snapshot. Editing the diary later leaves the shared copy as it was. Remove the update in Friends to withdraw it. New friends do not receive previous diary shares. Only the author sees total cheers, and each other reader sees only their own cheer. Challenge invitations and scores stay with the two participants. To move a diary from the offline edition, export it there, then choose Your account → Import a Dayplate backup online.

## Source and development

The full site uses React, TypeScript, Vinext, a Cloudflare Worker and D1. Apple export parsing uses the MIT-licensed fflate and the SAX parser with its license in vendor/xml. The source tree includes a separate offline entry point that runs entirely in-browser. Social features require the signed-in server.

Use Node 22.13 or newer.

```sh
npm ci
npm run build
node scripts/check-diary.mjs
node scripts/check-social.mjs
node scripts/check-signin.mjs
node portable/build.mjs outputs/Dayplate.html
```

For local development run `npm run dev`. Apply SQL migrations from `drizzle/` in filename order to the local D1 database. To run the built worker, use `npm start`. The hosted deployment uses the Sites sign-in gateway to authenticate requests and assign account IDs. Do not expose an independent Worker that trusts client-supplied identity headers. When adapting to another host, verify sessions server-side before supplying the owner ID.

## Configure Google sign-in

Register a Google OAuth Web application with the JavaScript origin `https://khandeparkaranmol-beep.github.io`. Set GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID in the Worker environment and apply the database migrations. GitHub Pages renders the Google button and diary. The API verifies the signed Google identity and exchanges a single-use challenge for a revocable session held in the browser tab. The offline diary is separate and stays in IndexedDB.

Build the Pages files from the project root with `node portable/build.mjs index.html pages` plus `node portable/build.mjs Dayplate.html` and `node portable/build.mjs share.html share`. Copy `portable/sw.js` alongside the HTML files for offline reopening.

### Buddy week

In Friends, two accepted friends can invite each other to a private seven-day habit challenge. Each sets a habit, a target number of local days, and a sharing preference. The week begins the next morning in each participant’s time zone. Check in once a day, reschedule, or ask for encouragement. Reminders appear in the journal and Friends while the site is open. Selected meals can be saved to My kitchen with their portions and nutrition. Head-to-head competitions remain available separately.

### Responsive UX audit

| Surface | What changed |
|---|---|
| Journal | Daily intake and meals come first. Marking a day complete produces a receipt with meal totals, selected nutrients and links back to the portions. |
| Foods | Search results appear above the expanded category list. Custom foods, package lookup and the data audit share a disclosure. My kitchen remains accessible from the planner. |
| Movement | Workout logging and movement challenges use the same activity and duration pickers. Saved sessions retain their duration and estimate. |
| Progress | The weekly review prioritizes readings from logged entries. It separates movement and nutrient averages, with supporting calculations available on demand. |
| Friends | Direct invitations show each person’s habit, days and sharing choice. Offline users have a route to sign-in. |
| Forms and navigation | Mobile uses bottom tabs, tablet uses a side rail, and desktop has top navigation with a two-column workspace. A form keeps its draft when the viewport changes. |

Browser checks covered 320 px and 390 px phones, an 820 px tablet, and a 1440 px desktop, using a test diary. Regression coverage includes nutrition, diary persistence, local dates through a daylight-saving transition, sharing and Apple Health file import. OAuth verification used synthetic keys. The browser checks did not use personal Google accounts or physical mobile devices.
